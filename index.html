A signal is a reason to check further. It is not proof of fraud. Some legitimate employers may trigger low-weight signals, and some scams may avoid obvious warning signs.

What to do next

    Share a privacy-safe summary

    The shared summary contains only the result labels and number of signals. It does not include the pasted job text, recruiter email, company URL, or checklist answers.

    Why this tool exists

    Job seekers are increasingly targeted through remote-work pitches, fake recruiter messages, fake checks, and “task” work that eventually asks the worker to deposit money. The FTC and FBI have published warnings about these patterns. (Federal Trade Commission, 2024, 2025, 2026; Federal Bureau of Investigation, Internet Crime Complaint Center, 2024.)

    01

    Verify independently

    Find the employer's real website yourself and confirm the role before relying on contact details supplied in a message.

    02

    Never pay to get paid

    Requests for money, crypto, gift cards, or check-funded equipment purchases deserve immediate scrutiny.

    03

    Protect your identity

    Do not rush to provide banking or identity information to someone who contacted you unexpectedly.
